SSRN Working Paper · Under Review · May 2026
The State λ Paradox: Programmable Friction, Welfare Targeting, and the Case for Dual-Track CBDC Design
This paper formalises programmable friction as λ ∈ [0,1], a continuous parameter measuring the degree of programmable constraint on a digital monetary token. Using a welfare framework W(λ) = U(λ) − C(λ), I show that the optimal λ differs sharply by income source: for directed welfare transfers, λ* > 0 because leakage reduction dominates; for private income, λ* → 0 absent user consent, because autonomy costs dominate. I call this the State λ Paradox.

Pilot survey evidence from India (N = 68 fielded, N = 34 attention-check filtered) finds that users accept self-imposed programmable budgeting at 3.74/5 but reject state-imposed restrictions at 2.66/5 (t = −4.11, p < 0.0001, Cohen's dz = 0.705). The gap is large enough to make a single CBDC design difficult to defend. I propose a Dual-Track architecture: a high-λ Welfare CBDC for government transfers, and a low-λ General CBDC with optional user-controlled friction — alongside governance prerequisites (CBDC-specific data protection, tiered anonymity, and independent programmability oversight) without which even a well-designed instrument risks replicating the surveillance risks its technical architecture creates.

The State λ Paradox

The welfare function W(λ) has a strictly positive optimum for directed welfare transfers and an optimum at or near zero for private earned income — because the autonomy cost C(λ) is calibrated differently across income sources by reference-point effects.

§ 5 · Design Proposal

Dual-Track Architecture

Since W(λ) has opposing optima for Ydirected and Yprivate, a single instrument cannot simultaneously achieve both. The solution is two separate instruments, not a compromise λ.

Track 1

Welfare CBDC

λ ≈ 0.7–1.0
Merchant whitelisting at registered Fair Price Shops
Time-decay expiry: δ(t) = e−αt active
Offline NFC capability for low-connectivity areas
Lower KYC threshold for small-value wallets
Empirical reference: Puducherry PMGKAY pilot (Feb 2026) ≈ λ 0.9 · Gujarat G-SAFAL ≈ λ 0.8
Track 2

General CBDC

λ → 0
State-imposed friction: λ = 0
Optional user-set λ ∈ [0, 0.3] — self-commitment tools
Tiered anonymity below ₹10,000/month
Coexists with — does not replace — UPI

Independent Programmability Oversight Board for λ > 0.3
Original Contributions

Three Theoretical Models

λ
Digital Envelope Model
A friction coefficient λ ∈ [0,1] parameterises the degree of programmable constraint on a CBDC token. The model derives household welfare W(λ) = U(λ) − C(λ), where U captures self-control gains from reduced leakage and C captures the autonomy cost of restriction. The welfare function is strictly concave, yielding a unique interior optimum λ* < 1 — meaning maximum programmability is never optimal, even for welfare transfers.
Formal Model · Welfare Economics
P
State λ Paradox
The optimal programmable friction λ* has opposing values depending on the income source. For directed welfare transfers (Ydirected), λ* > 0 because targeting efficiency gains outweigh autonomy costs. For private earned income (Yprivate), λ* → 0 absent explicit consent, because autonomy costs dominate. Survey evidence from India confirms this distinction at high statistical significance (p < 0.0001, dz = 0.705).
Core Result · Survey N=68
T
Dual-Track Architecture
The design implication is not a compromise λ, but two separate instruments. Track 1 — a high-λ Welfare CBDC (λ ≈ 0.7–1.0) for government transfers with merchant whitelisting and time-decay expiry. Track 2 — a low-λ General CBDC (λ ≈ 0) for private income with optional user-set friction. Both issue under a single legal framework with tiered anonymity and independent oversight.
